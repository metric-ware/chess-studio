User preferences as of 2026-10-07. Top priority is a hook that makes people keep watching; second is watch time: a fast cut where something happens at every moment, no empty or silent stretches, and no recap, replay, or outro at the end. They would rather get a 20s video than a 30s one padded with footage that adds nothing, even when they named 30s. They report that chess audiences like 2D/2.5D animated cutscenes as hooks (a knight jumping, a queen moving, a cinematic animation rather than a game on a board), along with good typography and fast editing that feels good. They disliked targeting circles beside pieces, generic hero shots, cheap effects, weak openings, extended endings, and a camera that is smooth but static. They want no 3D: Blender was uninstalled at their request and 2.5D is the maximum. They cite ZaveeCHESS and Strategy Chess as channels with good editing, and on 2026-10-07 asked to follow their theme colours (grey/white board on a dark backdrop, white text with one accent colour). They dislike a character/cartoon treatment of pieces (faces or eyes on pieces, brows, sweat drops, squash and stretch) and do not want that vibe anywhere, including the notes; pieces stay chess pieces. They disliked the Fraunces serif and cream/gold caption colours, and any glow on caption text (red words included). Latest feedback on 2026-10-07 preferred a simple fully black background over the charcoal treatment and still disliked the camera FOV and zooms, typography and animation. The resulting tooling revision uses one fixed 20-degree lens with span-based framing, wider compositions, anchored camera transitions, Inter Tight text, and cleaner caption animation; automatic zoom pulses, handheld noise, grain and bouncing captions were removed. In the follow-up, the user preferred the earlier cinematic knight movement and found the revised hook and board piece animation too simple. The next revision restored energetic rigid-piece jumps and landing reactions while retaining the longer lens and black background. They also disliked the hypothetical corner-mate explanation and rewind, preferring the real chase to continue immediately after the sacrifice and double check; that detour was removed and the cut shortened to 15.4s. For Shorts they want only the MP4: no thumbnail, title, or description work. Shorts are 4:5. The project keeps the latest three edits, each with a final-build record so later fix/polish requests start from the agreed version. Exported videos carry no identifying metadata (tools, encoders, sources). An edit request means an upload-ready video in one run with research, strong animation, and autonomous fixes; they don't want to say "keep going". They called tests and automated video-quality checks bloat that steers agents toward arbitrary metrics, and asked for them to be forbidden. They prefer aggressive cleanup of legacy code and docs. On 2026-10-09 they called the Légal trap's first cut decent but found its hook lighting too dark, objected to aqua/cyan used as the accent on every caption (it belongs to brilliant moves only; white, orange or violet were suggested), and felt the animation went by too fast; the revision lengthened it from 13.6s to 18.5s with a 5.2s hook and one move per beat.
