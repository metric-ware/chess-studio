studio/board.py renders a 2.5D perspective scene in board units (one unit per square, a1 corner at the origin, z up). Cam(x, y, span=12, yaw=0, pitch=70, roll=0, ox=0, oy=0) uses one fixed 20-degree vertical lens. span is the visible vertical extent at the target, replacing independently authored distance and FOV because that combination produced exaggerated close-ups and inconsistent zooms. View derives distance from span; yaw/pitch are degrees, roll is radians, offsets are pixels. Camera interpolation takes the shortest angular route. keyframes(t, keys) accepts (start, duration, Cam or fn(t)); each transition anchors its source at the start, estimates the incoming velocity over 1/120s, and eases toward the destination with quintic smoothstep. This avoids drifting transition origins and stopping a moving chase at its next transition.
Look defaults to a grey/white board (#f0f0f0/#999ca0) with a thin slab (0.12 squares), ambient 0.7 and a solid black background, with no floor or board fog. The latest user preference favoured this over the earlier charcoal tiled surroundings. Optional floor/floor2 checks support standing-piece cutscenes at z=0 when board=False; fog_density fades the floor and sprites toward background, also black, and accumulates only beyond fog_start (default 0). Fog from the camera darkened a subject about 11 units away by roughly 40%, which made the Légal trap hook look dark however strong its lights were; with fog_start just past the subject the pieces keep full brightness and light strengths need to drop accordingly, or the floor washes out and black pieces turn pale. The sky-gradient and slab-contact-shadow machinery was removed with the board surroundings.
Cairo supports affine transforms, so squares, highlights, arrows and trails are exactly projected polygons, while sprites use local fits. View.local uses central differences across a square. Flat pieces face +y on the board and follow yaw/roll; stand_matrix makes billboards face the camera. SPRITE_FOOT=0.097 is the transparent margin under neo sprites. Moving flat pieces cast a blurred silhouette shadow; standing pieces have a soft contact shadow. sx=-1 mirrors the chasing knight.
draw_board draws the background/optional floor, slab sides, board top and dark squares, under() decals, low-resolution lighting/fog multiplied and added with uint8 arrays, over() decals, optional depth of field, and pieces. Each square has its own move_to because connected subpaths previously filled triangular wedges. Flat sprites sort stably by z, standing sprites far to near. Surface depth of field precedes sprites; sprites blur by their own depth, since blurring the completed image smeared in-focus billboards using the depth of the floor behind them. Near-camera pieces are culled below depth 0.8 and sprite resolution is capped at 1024 to avoid huge allocations.
View.arrow uses square-cornered shafts and knight L paths; View.line is a round-capped board-plane stroke; View.glow is the radial check-square marker. Game validates SAN and schedules landing times. Game.pose(i, pr) returns x/y/z/rot: a short backward preparation, quintic travel, and a ballistic lift. Knights follow a quadratic curve through the L corner of their move at height 0.95 squares; kings lift 0.2 and other pieces 0.28. Pieces rotate slightly during flight and rock briefly over the first 0.2s after landing. Captures displace the victim 0.18 squares along the incoming direction and rotate/fade it under the capturer over 0.18s, without scaling. These stronger trajectories replaced the low, uniform glides after the user found the piece motion too simple. Check arrows last from landing until the reply lands, fading during the reply; mate arrows persist. trail(t, sym) returns the route and events() returns sounds. Game.pieces lists a moving piece first and then static pieces in python-chess piece_map order, which runs from h8 down to a1, so finding a piece by symbol alone returns the wrong one when two share it (the Légal trap's pin line once started at the f8 bishop); matching on the sq key avoids that.
